Premium Portfolio Website Revamp

Act as a world-class digital art director, senior UI/UX designer, and principal frontend engineer. Your task is to comprehensively redesign and refine this portfolio website into a premium, visually distinctive, production-grade digital experience.

1. Creative Direction & Visual Identity

Create an exceptionally polished, sophisticated, editorial-quality portfolio that feels custom-designed by an elite digital studio.

Establish a strong visual identity with deliberate typography, refined spacing, sophisticated composition, and excellent visual hierarchy.
Aim for understated luxury, modern editorial design, and intentional minimalism.
Use a cohesive design system across every page and component.
Prioritize visual storytelling, strong layouts, meaningful whitespace, subtle depth, and precise alignment.
Make every section feel intentional. Avoid generic templates, predictable layouts, excessive gradients, glassmorphism, unnecessary rounded cards, and decorative UI without purpose.
Avoid anything that makes the site look AI-generated, including generic copy, repetitive layouts, arbitrary floating elements, excessive animations, and predictable design patterns.
The result should feel bespoke, memorable, confident, and timeless rather than trendy. 2. Design & UX
Audit the entire existing website before making changes.
Improve every page, section, component, interaction, and responsive state.
Establish clear visual hierarchy and intuitive navigation.
Make content easy to scan while maintaining a strong aesthetic.
Design thoughtful hover states, transitions, loading states, and micro-interactions.
Ensure interactions provide meaningful feedback.
Maintain consistency without making every section visually repetitive.
Preserve the site's existing content, functionality, and brand identity unless improvements are clearly justified. Do not invent projects, credentials, or achievements. 3. Motion & Interaction

The project already uses GSAP, WebGL, Lenis, and Swiper. Leverage these existing technologies intelligently rather than introducing unnecessary dependencies.

GSAP: Use for refined entrance animations, scroll-triggered reveals, staggered sequences, and carefully orchestrated transitions.
WebGL: Preserve and enhance existing WebGL experiences where they genuinely add value. Keep them performant and provide graceful fallbacks.
Lenis: Ensure smooth scrolling works seamlessly with GSAP ScrollTrigger and native browser behavior.
Swiper: Refine sliders, carousels, and touch interactions with responsive configurations and intuitive controls.
Prioritize subtle, purposeful motion over spectacle.
Avoid animation overload, unnecessary parallax, scroll hijacking, and effects that interfere with usability.
Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
Ensure animations never block content visibility, navigation, or interaction. 4. Engineering & Performance

Maintain the existing Next.js architecture and work with the current stack.

Inspect the project structure, dependencies, existing components, and implementation before modifying anything.
Preserve working functionality and avoid unnecessary rewrites.
Build reusable, maintainable, well-structured components.
Follow Next.js best practices, including appropriate Server and Client Component boundaries.
Minimize unnecessary client-side JavaScript, hydration, and re-renders.
Optimize images, fonts, video, WebGL assets, and other heavy resources.
Use dynamic imports and lazy loading where appropriate.
Prevent layout shifts and eliminate unnecessary render-blocking resources.
Avoid installing new dependencies unless there is a compelling technical reason.
Eliminate console errors, broken interactions, and performance bottlenecks.

Performance is a design requirement, not an afterthought.

5. Responsive Design

Deliver a genuinely responsive experience across:

Large desktop displays.
Standard laptops.
Tablets.
Mobile devices, including narrow screens.

Do not simply scale down the desktop layout. Reconsider composition, typography, spacing, navigation, media, and interactions for smaller screens. Ensure touch targets, gestures, and interactive elements work naturally on mobile.

6. Accessibility

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA standards wherever applicable.

Use semantic HTML and appropriate landmarks.
Ensure complete keyboard accessibility and visible focus states.
Maintain sufficient color contrast.
Provide accessible navigation, forms, buttons, and interactive components.
Include appropriate alt text and accessible labels.
Never communicate information through color alone.
Ensure animations and scrolling remain accessible.
Make all interactive experiences usable without a mouse. 7. Implementation Workflow

Work autonomously and comprehensively. Minimize unnecessary clarification requests and avoid stopping after producing a superficial redesign.

Audit the entire codebase and understand the existing design, architecture, and functionality.
Identify visual inconsistencies, UX problems, technical debt, responsiveness issues, and performance bottlenecks.
Establish a coherent design direction and implement it consistently.
Refactor and redesign components where necessary without breaking existing functionality.
Review every page and section, not just the homepage.
Test desktop, tablet, and mobile layouts.
Verify animations, scrolling, navigation, sliders, and WebGL behavior.
Run available linting, type checks, and production builds. Fix all issues introduced by your changes.
Perform a final visual and accessibility quality audit. 8. Quality Bar & Non-Negotiables

The finished website must:

Look like a premium, professionally art-directed portfolio.
Feel bespoke rather than template-generated.
Be visually cohesive across every page.
Load quickly and remain responsive under real-world conditions.
Have polished interactions without sacrificing usability.
Work flawlessly across modern browsers and device sizes.
Be accessible, maintainable, and production-ready.
Preserve all existing essential functionality.

Do not settle for the first acceptable implementation. Critically evaluate your own design decisions and refine weak areas before declaring completion.

Execution rule: Make the actual changes directly in the codebase. Do not merely provide recommendations, mockups, or a list of proposed improvements. Do not leave placeholder implementations or TODOs. Prioritize complete, high-quality execution over unnecessary explanations.

The final deliverable should be a cohesive, production-ready portfolio that looks and feels like the work of an exceptional independent digital designer and engineering team.
